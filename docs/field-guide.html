    Read this first

    The essentials

    Program the issued drone to fly an indoor obstacle course on its own, with no GPS and no pilot input: through a maze, a dark tunnel, a chicane, and onto a landing pad. Every team gets the same drone. The weekend is for writing the software that flies it.

    The organisers' Challenge page states the scoring outright: every finish flown below the walls scores above every unfinished run. That one line decides the strategy.

    Our strategy in six lines

    1. Finish first, then go faster. Corners set the time, not top speed.
    2. Use the kit's software wherever it costs nothing: driver, cuVSLAM, bridge, PX4.
    3. Add our code only where simulation measured a failure: the guard, the planner, the follower, the map correction.
    4. Plan the route from the course map, never hand-drawn waypoints: generality is 15 points and an AI review flags hard-coding.
    5. Survey the real course against the published file, to about 5 cm, before trusting the map.
    6. Never touch the organisers' PX4 safety settings (rule 8.5).

    From the Challenge page

    Scoring and awards

    The run, 60 points

    • Unfinished: checkpoint points reached + up to 7 for speed: 7 × fastest time to your last checkpoint ÷ yours. Reaching no checkpoint scores 0.
    • Finished: 35 checkpoint points + 20 × fastest finish ÷ yours + 5 through the window.
    • Return bonus: run points × 1.25 for taking off again, flying over the barrier, and landing within 2 ft of the launch mark, all inside 3 minutes. A miss on the return loses only the bonus.
    • Penalties: +5 s per touch of a wall, tunnel or boulder. A crash, touching the ground elsewhere, leaving the course or the 3-minute limit ends the run.
    • Over a wall: that checkpoint earns 15%, and the run can never be the fastest time or win Fastest Run.
    • Times run from leaving the launch mark. Ties go to the faster run.

    What the numbers say

    Each checkpoint is worth more than any amount of speed before it. A finish through the window with the return is worth about 1.25 × (40 + up to 20). Speed is the last 20 points, and Fastest Run is the smallest prize. The judged 40 is decided by the pitch and the code, so the evidence for every design decision matters as much as the flying.

    Guard

    cuVSLAM answers confidently on a blank view: on a grey panel it reports hundreds of features and a still camera, so PX4 thinks the drone stopped and keeps accelerating. The guard withholds a frame when the image has under 1% texture, when tracking failed, when it implies over 10 m/s or 720°/s, or when its rotation disagrees with the gyro. When vision returns, it continues from PX4's own estimate so there is no jump. It writes fixed, honest variances, because cuVSLAM's covariance is not an uncertainty.

    Planner

    Slice the course model at 0.8 m into wall segments, add the net and the boulders (widened by the rangefinder's footprint), and run A* on a 5 cm grid with Nav2's inflation cost: impassable within 0.32 m of a wall, then an exponential penalty. Goals in order: the tunnel's middle, the window, the pad. Then shortcut stretches where a straight line is no riskier. About 17 m from the launch mark to the pad.

    Follower

    Slide a setpoint along the route with an arc at each corner, a speed profile from PX4's own acceleration and jerk limits, and the nose pointed 0.8 m ahead so the camera never faces a corridor's end wall. Through a pose-only interface, lead the pose by the velocity and acceleration PX4 would otherwise lag: pose + v/0.95 + a/(0.95 × 1.8), at 50 Hz.

    Map correction

    Cut a band of stereo depth at flight height into a 2D scan, match it to the course walls (an iterated Kalman filter with ICP), and correct the pose by centimetres. It also estimates each wall unit's offset, like AlphaPilot estimating gate placement, and the planner re-plans on the result. Only safe on a course laid as published: check with a tape first.

    Frames, before anything flies

    A 90° mistake flies into a wall. Get these right on the bench, props off.

    • PX4 is NED / FRD (x forward, y right, z down). ROS is ENU / FLU (x forward, y left, z up). PX4 timestamps are microseconds.
    • The tracker's frame starts at the body resting on the launch mark, facing north up the first corridor.
    • Course point (east, north) to setpoint: subtract the launch mark (−2.515, 0.914); rotate by −90° so north becomes forward; height = 0.8 m above the floor minus the body's resting height (tape-measured).
    • Carry test: carry the drone 1 m forward and check which axis of /fmu/out/vehicle_local_position grows. Then the same for setpoints, props off.
    ROS 2 traps on this stack
    • QoS: PX4's /fmu/out/* topics are best-effort. A default subscriber silently receives nothing.
    • DDS domain: ROS_DOMAIN_ID must match PX4's UXRCE_DDS_DOM_ID (their file sets 1).
    • Message versions: px4_msgs must match the firmware; on 1.16 some topics carry _v1. Use the kit's, never our own.
    • Big images: Fast DDS's default 512 KB shared-memory segment drops 1 MB best-effort images measured.

    The 10-point checkpoint

    The dark tunnel

    Why it may be less blind than it sounds inferred

    The tube is about 1.5 m wide, 1.8 m tall and 1.5 m long. From the entrance, the lit exit spans about 52° of the camera's 80° view; from the middle, more than the whole frame. A camera pointed along the axis sees the exit the whole way. The real risk is auto-exposure metering the dark walls and burning out the exit, or the drone turning inside.

    What we do

    • No turning inside the tube; fly its axis.
    • Cap auto-exposure around 8–10 ms and meter on the image centre: keeps the exit exposed and blur 4× lower.
    • Stamp frames at mid-exposure (i_add_exposure_offset).
    • If vision drops anyway, the guard withholds and EKF2 coasts on the IMU and rangefinder for the ~1.5 m, then re-anchors at the exit. Our simulator's tunnel shows the lens nothing, and the course still clears measured.
    • Rule 3.4: no light may be added. A sensor's own illuminator is a question for an organiser.

    Questions to expect, with our answers

    How does the drone know where it is in the dark tunnel?

    The camera sees the lit exit for most of the tube if we fly its axis and cap exposure. If vision drops, the guard withholds the frames rather than passing cuVSLAM's confident "standing still", PX4 coasts on its IMU with the rangefinder for height across about 1.5 m, and when vision returns the guard continues from PX4's estimate so there is no jump. In simulation with the tunnel fully blinded, the course still cleared.

    Is your route hard-coded?

    No. A* plans it on a map sliced from the published course model, with Nav2's inflation cost. The goals (tunnel, window, pad) come from the map. With the boulders moved at random it re-planned 20 of 20 layouts clear, where a hand-drawn route failed 14.

    What would change on a different course?

    The map file. The planner, follower, guard and estimator settings are course-agnostic. The layout filter estimates each wall unit's actual placement against the map, and the planner re-plans on what it finds.

    Why stereo-only cuVSLAM instead of visual-inertial?

    In simulation, cuVSLAM's inertial mode initialises its IMU in flight and blocks tracking while it does: one break per run, against 5 of 5 clean in stereo. PX4's EKF2 already fuses the Pixhawk's IMU with vision, which is the inertial fusion we need. NVIDIA also warns that Python inertial odometry on a Jetson may not be reliable.

    What happens when tracking fails mid-run?

    The guard withholds the bad poses; PX4 dead-reckons; vision re-anchors onto PX4's estimate when it returns; after 30 withheld frames the tracker restarts. If setpoints stop, PX4 holds position after 1 s. We do not touch the organisers' failsafes (rule 8.5), and a teammate is always on the kill switch.

    Why not SLAM with loop closure?

    The course never revisits a place, so there is nothing to close a loop on, and a loop closure arrives as a jump that the flight controller dislikes. Rule 6.4 also forbids carrying a map from one run to the next. We use the published map instead, which is allowed.

    How did you test it?

    A PX4 software-in-the-loop simulator of the published course, with cuVSLAM on simulated stereo, run as A/B comparisons over many random layouts per change. Every design decision has a dated table behind it. Then the organisers' lab, the bench checks, and the real drone's logs.

    Why not fly faster?

    The kit is capped at 1.0 m/s, and in simulation 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 m/s all took the same 27–31 s, because the legs are short and corners set the time. 1.0 was the cleanest. Reliability wins points; speed is the last 20.

    Did you add sensors? Why not?

    We modelled a 2D lidar in simulation: once deskewed it localised as well as stereo, no better. The organisers discourage added sensors because wiring risks the props. So none, unless the real tunnel turns out too dark for the camera.

    Which parts did you write, and which did you use?

    Ours: the guard, the planner, the follower and, if time allows, the map correction. Used and credited: the organisers' starter project, NVIDIA cuVSLAM and Isaac ROS, Luxonis DepthAI and depthai-ros, PX4, ROS 2, Nav2's cost formulas, SciPy, OpenCV, trimesh, Gazebo. AI tools helped write code, as rule 9.2 allows; we can walk through every line.

    Terms the judges will use

    Glossary

    VO / VIO / SLAMVisual odometry adds up camera motion and drifts. VIO adds an IMU for fast motion and short dropouts. SLAM adds a map and loop closure, correcting drift when it recognises a place.
    cuVSLAMNVIDIA's GPU visual odometry and SLAM library; about 2–4 ms a frame on a Jetson.
    EKF2PX4's extended Kalman filter: fuses the IMU with vision, rangefinder and barometer into one estimate.
    External vision (EV)PX4's input for position from outside, here the camera, as VehicleOdometry on /fmu/in/vehicle_visual_odometry.
    Offboard modePX4 flies setpoints sent by the companion computer; it needs a steady heartbeat of at least 2 Hz.
    uXRCE-DDSHow PX4 talks ROS 2: a client in PX4 and an agent on the Jetson make PX4's topics appear as ROS topics.
    NED / FRD, ENU / FLUPX4: north-east-down, forward-right-down. ROS: east-north-up, forward-left-up.
    SITLSoftware in the loop: the real PX4 code flying a simulated drone.
    Inflation costNav2's way of making cells near walls expensive, so A* keeps to the middle of corridors.
    Re-anchoringWhen vision restarts with a new origin, shifting its poses so they continue from the flight controller's estimate instead of jumping.
    DeskewingMoving each lidar beam back by the drone's motion during the scan's turn.
    Global shutterThe whole image is captured at once, so motion doesn't warp it; the OAK-D S2's stereo pair has it.
