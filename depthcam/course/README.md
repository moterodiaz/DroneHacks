# Course tools: STEP → map → Gazebo world → planned trajectory → flight → analysis

These scripts take the organisers' STEP model of the course and produce:

- a map the code can read;
- a Gazebo world for PX4 SITL;
- the fastest trajectory that keeps the whole drone plus a safety margin clear of everything, as 50 Hz PX4 setpoints;
- a player that flies that trajectory with PX4 offboard control;
- an analysis of the flight log: checkpoint times, tracking error, wall clearance.

The map, world and planner need only numpy and OpenCV, and run the same on the Windows laptop, WSL2 and the Jetson. The players need pymavlink (MAVLink) or ROS 2 with px4_msgs.

```
python course/12_extract_course_map.py course/maps/course_v3.step   # -> course/maps/course_v3_map.json
python course/13_make_gazebo_world.py                                # -> course/gazebo/{models,worlds}
python course/14_plan_course.py                                      # -> course/out/<route>_yaw<deg>_*
bash course/gazebo/run_sitl.sh                                       # (WSL2) Gazebo + PX4 SITL on the course
python3 course/15_fly_plan.py course/out/window_yaw90_px4_ned.csv --gcs-heartbeat   # (WSL2) fly it
python course/16_analyze_flight.py course/out/flights/<log folder>   # times, tracking, clearance, picture
python course/17_lidar_study.py course/out/flights/<log folder>      # what smear and tilt do to MS200K localisation
python3 course/18_lidar_live.py check --port /dev/ms200              # the real MS200K: check, record, localize
python3 course/19_lidar_px4_bridge.py --port /dev/ms200              # lidar pose -> PX4 (external vision)

```

## Frame

The map, the Gazebo world and the `_enu.csv` setpoints share one frame:

- **Units and axes:** metres, +x east, +y north, +z up.
- **Origin:** the centre of the floor.
- **z:** the height of the drone's body centre.

The `_px4_ned.csv` file holds the same setpoints in PX4 local NED:

- **Origin:** the launch mark, at the drone's resting height.
- **Conversion:** north = y − y_launch, east = x − x_launch, down = −(z − rest).

This is the PX4 local frame in SITL when the drone is spawned on the launch mark, and in flight if your localisation reports positions relative to the launch mark.

## The rules file

Checkpoints are gates: segments the drone's centre crosses, in order. A gate can be given as:

- `segment`: two points.
- `between`: the clear gap between two named features at flight height (Gate A is `["M3 maze divider", "Net side north"]`).
- `opening`: a wall's window.
- `tube_end`: one end of a tube.

A checkpoint with `routes` offers alternatives (`window`, `gate_b`); `14` plans each.

`extra_margin_m` adds clearance around named features. The v3 file asks for 10 cm more around the tube, because the camera VIO goes blind in there. That is a planning choice, not a rule.

What the organisers told us, and how the file encodes it:

- **CP1:** "follow roughly the floor line", so CP1 is the line's turn east, across the corridor.
- **Clock:** starts when the drone leaves the launch mark and stops at disarm on the pad. The planner counts from liftoff.
- **Touches:** each touch of a wall, the tunnel or a boulder adds 5 s.
- **Lights:** none allowed on the drone.
- **Return:** after disarming on the pad, the drone takes off unaided, crosses the barrier, and lands within 2 ft of the launch mark inside the 3-minute limit. A failed return loses only the bonus.

## How the planner works (`course/planner/`)

1. **Configuration space** (`cspace.py`).
   - The drone is a rectangle, 0.40 m nose to tail and 0.50 m wide by default, grown by the margin.
   - It holds one heading for the whole run (default nose north).
   - Every obstacle at the cruise height band is grown by that rectangle (a Minkowski sum). The drone is then a point, and "the point is outside every grown polygon" means "the whole drone plus margin is clear".
   - Openings count only where the body's height band fits through them; the window does at 0.8 m.
   - Boulders count at any height: flying over one would make the downward rangefinder see the floor jump 0.5 m.
2. **Route** (`route.py`): A* on a 2 cm grid from the launch mark through each gate to the pad, then line-of-sight shortcuts.
3. **Smoothing** (`smooth.py`).
   - Trades path length against curvature.
   - Gate points slide along their gates, and obstacles are a hard constraint, checked at the points and at the segment midpoints.
   - Run for several trade-off weights.
4. **Speed** (`speed.py`).
   - Time-optimal speed along each candidate for a point mass with a total acceleration limit (forward/backward pass), capped at `--v-max`.
   - This is the same answer TOPP-RA gives for these constraints.
   - The fastest candidate wins.
5. **Assembly** (`plan.py`): vertical takeoff, the course, landing with a slow last 25 cm, the disarm delay, and the return at 2.3 m over the barrier.

## Results on the v3 course (defaults: margin 5 cm, a_max 3 m/s², v_max 2.5 m/s, nose north, 0.8 m)

| Route | Finish (liftoff → disarm) | Bonus | Tightest gate for the drone centre |
|---|---|---|---|
| window | 15.4 s | +5 | window 0.34 m |
| gate_b | 16.9 s | 0 | Gate A 0.42 m (also a 0.865 m slanted channel between C1 and C2b) |

- **Window route:** about 1.5 s faster than Gate B and worth 5 more points, because its turns are gentler.
- **Heading:** nose east (`--yaw-deg 0`) is about 0.1 s faster but has less room: Gate A 0.32 m and the window 0.24 m.
- **Margin use:** the fastest path uses the whole margin at the inside of every corner (3–7 mm beyond it). At speed, tracking error will exceed 5 cm. Start slower and with a bigger margin, for example `--a-max 1.5 --v-max 1.2 --margin 0.10`, and tighten from flight logs.

## Gazebo (`13_make_gazebo_world.py`, `gazebo/run_sitl.sh`)

How each part is modelled:

- **Walls:** boxes, split around the window.
- **Tube:** a generated OBJ for the visual and a ring of boxes for collision.
- **Boulders:** dome visuals with cylinder collision.
- **Nets:** collision only.
- **Floor markings:** one texture.
- **Tube cradles:** left out (the tube is static).

Like PX4's own worlds, the world lists no system plugins, so PX4's `server.config` supplies them.

`run_sitl.sh` starts Gazebo on the world from the repo, spawns the drone, and lets PX4 attach to both. Nothing is copied into the PX4 tree.

- **Default drone:** `x500_course`, the x500 without propeller collisions (see "Simulator drone" above).
- **With the 2D lidar:** `DRONE=x500_course_lidar_2d`. It needs GPU rendering; under WSL2 on the laptop it runs at 3% of real time.
- **PX4's stock model, spawned by PX4:** `DRONE=gz_x500`.

## Flying a plan (`player.py`, `15_fly_plan.py`, `ros2/plan_player_node.py`)

`course/player.py` is the state machine, with no transport in it:

1. Wait for a position.
2. Refuse to start if the drone is more than 30 cm from the plan's start.
3. Stream setpoints, then switch to offboard and arm.
4. Play the run against PX4's clock. That keeps playback in step with a slow simulator.
5. Touch down by commanding a 0.7 m/s descent with no height target, which is what PX4's land detector needs. Then disarm.
6. Wait 2 s, then fly the return the same way.

Two safety and timing behaviours:

- **Abort:** if the tracking error passes `--abort-error` (0.5 m, doubled during takeoff and landing), the player stops streaming and commands Land.
- **Takeoff catch-up:** during takeoff the plan clock waits while the drone is more than 10 cm below its setpoint. PX4 spools up and ramps its climb in on its own schedule.

Two thin front ends feed it:

- **`15_fly_plan.py`:** MAVLink via pymavlink, using `SET_POSITION_TARGET_LOCAL_NED` with position, velocity and acceleration feed-forward. Tested in SITL. It also works from the Jetson over a serial MAVLink link.
- **`ros2/plan_player_node.py`:** px4_msgs over uXRCE-DDS, the kit's stack. It subscribes to both `vehicle_local_position` and `vehicle_local_position_v1` (and the same for `vehicle_status`), because PX4 v1.16+ adds `_v1` to versioned messages. **Not run yet**: the laptop has no ROS 2. Its field names were checked against PX4 v1.17's message definitions.

Both write `flight_log.csv` and `flight_events.json` into `course/out/flights/<plan>_<time>/`.

`16_analyze_flight.py` reads those files and reports:

- **Checkpoint times:** measured from the start of the climb, against the planned times.
- **Off-path deviation:** distance to the planned path. This is what eats the margin.
- **Lag:** distance to the setpoint of the same instant.
- **Clearance:** the closest the real drone outline came to any obstacle, and every touch.
- **Landing accuracy:** offset from the pad centre and from the launch mark.

It also writes `report.json` and `flight.png`.

### Simulator drone

PX4's x500 has collision boxes on its spinning propellers. They make its footprint about 0.63 m square, larger than our drone, so its blades would clip walls our drone clears.

`gazebo/make_sim_drone.py` copies the x500 with those propeller collisions removed (`x500_course`). `run_sitl.sh` spawns it, and PX4 attaches with the stock x500 airframe. Clearance for the real 0.40 × 0.50 m drone is judged by the analysis, not by Gazebo contacts.

### SITL results (2026-10-28, PX4 v1.17, x500 with stock tuning, window route)

| Plan | Flown | Planned | Off-path p95 / max | Closest approach of the drone outline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| margin 5 cm, a_max 3, v_max 2.5 | 20.5 s | 15.4 s | 0.20 / 0.24 m | +2 mm (one run); −2.8 cm, a touch, then abort (another run) |
| same, played at half speed (`--slow 2`) | — | — | 0.10 / 0.12 m | +2.0 cm |
| margin 10 cm, a_max 1.5, v_max 2.0 | 28.8 s | 21.9 s | 0.19 / 0.27 m | +1.6 cm |

What the runs show:

- **The course part flies close to plan.** CP1 → CP4 took within about 0.4 s of the planned time.
- **The ends lose about 5 s.**
  - PX4's takeoff ramp (`MPC_TKO_RAMP_T`, default 3 s) costs about 3.5 s from liftoff to CP1. One run with `MPC_TKO_RAMP_T 0.5` saved about 2.6 s.
  - Waiting for the land detector costs about 1.4 s. `--disarm force` disarms as soon as the drone is on the floor and still.
- **A 5 cm margin at full speed is a coin flip.**
  - The stock x500 strays 20 cm sideways (p95).
  - About 12 cm is the most margin the course allows at a fixed nose-north heading. At 15 cm the leg from the tunnel to the window has no route.
  - Size `--margin` from your own drone's off-path p95 and tune PX4's position controller. These numbers are for the simulated x500, not your 7-inch drone.
- **The feed-forward really reaches PX4.** PX4's own log shows `offboard_control_mode` with position, velocity and acceleration all enabled, and the feed-forward values present.

## 2D lidar: Oradar MS200K

The MS200K is allowed at the event. It sweeps one horizontal plane at 5–15 Hz (about 4500 points/s, up to 12 m). Against the known course map, each sweep gives x, y and yaw. Height still comes from the downward rangefinder.

### Wiring

Connect it to the **Jetson**, not the Pixhawk.

- PX4 has no driver for it, and scan matching needs the Jetson anyway. The Jetson sends the pose to PX4 as external-vision odometry, over MAVLink on TELEM2 through mavlink-router (`19_lidar_px4_bridge.py`).
- Use the lidar's own USB-serial adapter in a Jetson USB port (230400 baud).
  - Add a udev rule so it always appears as `/dev/ms200`, not whichever `ttyUSB` number it gets next to the camera:
    1. Plug it in.
    2. Run `udevadm info -a -n /dev/ttyUSB0 | grep -m3 -E 'idVendor|idProduct|serial'`.
    3. Write `/etc/udev/rules.d/99-ms200.rules` with the values you read:

       ```
       SUBSYSTEM=="tty", ATTRS{idVendor}=="<vid>", ATTRS{idProduct}=="<pid>", SYMLINK+="ms200", MODE="0666"
       ```

       Add `ATTRS{serial}=="<serial>"` if another adapter on the drone uses the same chip.
    4. Run `sudo udevadm control --reload-rules && sudo udevadm trigger`.
  - Give the cable strain relief.
- The Jetson's 40-pin UART (3.3 V, `/dev/ttyTHS*`) also works without the adapter, but only if the Pixhawk link isn't already using it.
- Power: 5 V from the Jetson's USB is normally enough. Check the draw on the datasheet, since the OAK-D shares the same budget.
- Mount it on top, with the scan plane above the propellers and nothing tall in the way. Measure the plane's height above the body centre (the study assumes 0.10 m).

### The ROS driver (`MS200_Ros-V1.3.4`: package `oradar_lidar`, node `oradar_scan`)

To build for ROS 2:
1. Set `COMPILE_METHOD` to `COLCON` at the top of `CMakeLists.txt`.
2. Copy `package_ros2.xml` to `package.xml`.
3. Run `colcon build`.

The node publishes `sensor_msgs/LaserScan`. Its parameters:

| Parameter | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `port_name` | `/dev/ttyUSB0` | change to the udev name |
| `baudrate` | 230400 | |
| `motor_speed` | 10 | rotation in Hz, 5–15 |
| `scan_topic` | `MS200/scan` | |
| `frame_id` | `laser_frame` | |
| `range_min` | 0.05 m | raise to about 0.25 m so the drone's own arms are ignored |
| `range_max` | 20 m | |
| `angle_min` / `angle_max` | 0 / 360° | |
| `clockwise` | false | |

Catches:

1. **The ROS 2 launch file is Foxy-era.** Both `Node`s use `node_executable` / `node_name`, which Humble rejects. That includes the static `base_link → laser_frame` transform, which assumes the lidar sits 0.18 m above `base_link`.
   - **Fixed in `ros2/ms200_scan.launch.py`:** `ros2 launch course/ros2/ms200_scan.launch.py port:=/dev/ms200 z:=0.10`. It also sets `range_min` to 0.25 m. Not run yet.
2. **The node throws away the timing that scan-smear correction needs.**
   - The sensor timestamps every 12-point packet (about 2.7 ms apart), but the node discards those stamps.
   - The scan's stamp is when the node started waiting for the sweep, not when the sweep happened. `time_increment` comes from how long that wait took, which is near zero if a sweep was already queued.
   - It re-bins the points onto an even angle grid, which moves each one by up to 0.4°.
   - With `clockwise: false`, the per-point times implied by index order run backwards.
   - The "stock-driver timing" rows below show the cost.
   - Use the vendor node only to look at scans in rviz2. The localiser reads the sensor itself (next section).

### Our reader and the live localiser (`ms200.py`, `18_lidar_live.py`)

`ms200.py` reads the serial stream directly, using the frame format from the vendor SDK:
- 47-byte frames of 12 points;
- a 16-bit ms timestamp per frame;
- a CRC-8 with polynomial 0x4D, which is the SDK's table.

It returns each revolution with:
- every point's own time on the host clock;
- the exact angle, converted to our body frame (CCW from the nose, using the mounting offset);
- the range, with NaN for no return;
- the confidence.

How it gets the times:
- The sensor clock is mapped to the host clock through the least-delayed frame of the last 2 s.
- Each revolution uses one offset, so a scan never has a step inside it.
- Points within a frame are spaced by the rotation speed.
- The 30000 and 65536 timestamp wraps are both handled, and a sensor restart is detected.

Unverified, and to measure on the real sensor:
- **Which point the frame stamp belongs to.** Assumed to be the first. If it is the last, every time is 2.4 ms early.
- **The fixed sensor-to-host delay.** `--latency-ms` defaults to 5 ms. A constant error here shifts all times alike; PX4's `EKF2_EV_DELAY` absorbs it.

`lidar_localize.Tracker` runs the matcher revolution after revolution:
- the prior is the last pose moved at constant velocity;
- a match that fails, or jumps more than 30 cm, is replaced by the prediction and counted as lost;
- velocity for deskew comes from the last two poses, or from PX4 when given.

```
pip install pyserial                                          # on the Jetson (and pymavlink for --mavlink)
python3 course/18_lidar_live.py check --port /dev/ms200       # rate, points/turn, CRC errors, clock jitter, nearest return
python3 course/18_lidar_live.py record --port /dev/ms200 --seconds 30 -o walk.ms200
python3 course/18_lidar_live.py localize --port /dev/ms200    # from the launch mark, nose north; prints x, y, yaw
python3 course/18_lidar_live.py localize --replay walk.ms200 --csv walk.csv
python3 course/18_lidar_live.py localize --port /dev/ms200 --mavlink /dev/ttyTHS1   # + PX4 roll/pitch and velocity
```

First time with the sensor:
- `check` with a box in front of the nose prints the `--yaw-offset` to use.
- A box on the left must read about +90°. If it reads −90°, add `--counter-clockwise` (the sensor is mounted upside down).
- Pass the same two options to `record` and `localize`.

Bytes to pose, on synthetic streams (sensor frames from `lidar_sim`, arriving late and jittery, four frames per read):
- Times come back within 1 ms (the sensor's stamp resolution), angles exact, ranges to 0.5 mm.
- On the SITL flights (realistic / stress), position error RMS (max):
  - with the tracker's own velocity: 1.3 cm (2.4) / 2.6 cm (7.0);
  - with PX4's velocity: 0.5 cm (1.3) / 1.0 cm (4.6).
  - So fly with `--mavlink`.
- A replayed hand walk with the sensor 10° off the nose: 1.2 cm RMS, heading within 0.6°.

Not done:
- Running any of this on the sensor.
- `--mavlink` against a real PX4.

### Lidar → PX4 (`px4_link.py`, `19_lidar_px4_bridge.py`, `gz_truth.py`)

`19_lidar_px4_bridge.py` sends every lidar pose to PX4 as external vision. EKF2 then has horizontal position and heading with no GPS and no magnetometer, so PX4 can fly the plan indoors. Height stays with the barometer, helped by the rangefinder.

```
python3 course/19_lidar_px4_bridge.py --print-params                              # what PX4 needs
python3 course/19_lidar_px4_bridge.py --port /dev/ms200 --mavlink udpin:0.0.0.0:14541   # on the drone
python3 course/19_lidar_px4_bridge.py --sim                                       # SITL: lidar from Gazebo's true pose
```

How it works:
- **The message.** MAVLink ODOMETRY in `MAV_FRAME_LOCAL_NED`, where "north" is the course's +y and the origin is the plan's `px4_ned_origin_enu`. The planner's PX4 setpoints therefore need no change.
- **Timing.** The bridge answers PX4's TIMESYNC requests, so PX4 converts the measurement times to its own clock. That needs a link in onboard mode, and it converges after about 50 s.
- **Map slice by height.** The tracker matches against the map slice at the scan's height, and only against beams that end within that slice's band:
  - near the floor it sees the boulders and the tube cradles;
  - above the 1.73 m walls it sees only the perimeter nets;
  - beams within 5 cm of the wall tops are dropped, because they could be either.
- **Deskew and tilt** use PX4's velocity, attitude and gyro yaw rate.
- **Trust.** A revolution that jumps more than 30 cm or 5.7° from the prediction is not sent.

PX4 v1.17 behaviours this had to work around, all found in its source or in SITL:

| What | Consequence |
|---|---|
| ODOMETRY with `estimator_type` LIDAR is rejected ("unsupported") | the bridge sends VISION |
| x, y and z must all be finite, and the quaternion must be unit | z carries PX4's own height (EV height is not fused) |
| `EKF2_EVP_NOISE` / `EKF2_EVA_NOISE` (default 0.1) are floors on the vision noise | set to 0.02, or a 1 cm lidar is trusted as 10 cm |
| EKF2 fuses on a horizon `EKF2_DELAY_MAX` in the past, and `EKF2_GPS_DELAY` (110 ms) counts even with GPS off | set `EKF2_GPS_DELAY` 0; PX4 then raises `EKF2_DELAY_MAX` to 100 itself (because of `EKF2_ASP_DELAY`), horizon 156 → 72 ms |
| With the barometer as height reference, PX4's z on the floor is not 0 (+0.69 m on one SITL boot, about 0 on another) | the player measures the floor height on the ground before each takeoff and flies the plan's heights above it; the bridge takes height relative to the last "landed" |
| Height default `EKF2_HGT_REF` 1 is GPS | 0 (barometer) |

The gentle plan (10 cm margin, a_max 1.5, v_max 2.0) flew on the simulated lidar alone, with GPS and magnetometer off. The full run and the return over the barrier both completed in all three flights. Errors are measured against Gazebo's truth:

|---|---|---|
| Lidar pose, run, RMS / p95 / max | 1.1 / 1.6 / 9.9 cm | 0.9 / 1.6 / 5.0 cm |
| Lidar heading, p95 / max | 0.6° / 1.6° | 0.7° / 1.6° |
| Pose sent, delay after the measurement, p50 / p99 | 19 / 42 ms | 18 / 37 ms |
| PX4's position vs truth, RMS / p95 / max | 4.4 / 8.5 / 10.0 cm | 4.0 / 7.4 / 9.3 cm |
| Off-path p95 (truth) | 0.21 m | 0.22 m |
| Closest approach of the drone outline (truth) | −3.2 cm (north net, after CP1) | −2.0 cm |
| Run, liftoff to disarm (planned 21.9 s) | 30.3 s | 30.8 s |

- **The return:** lidar error 0.7 cm RMS, 4.8 cm max. 5 of its 118 revolutions weren't trusted, all while crossing the wall-top height.
- **What limits accuracy:** the lidar holds about 1 cm, but PX4's fused position is 4 cm RMS. With only 10 Hz position updates and no velocity measurement, EKF2 coasts on the IMU between scans. Each new scan lands up to 4.6 cm from its prediction (p95).
- **The touches are a control problem:** the stock x500 strays 0.2 m from the path, the same as it did on GPS.
- **Fixed along the way:**
  - Above the walls, a nets-only map slice was matched against wall hits. That gave 18 cm / 28° outliers, and the tracker's own differenced yaw rate then oscillated for several revolutions. The fix: height bands, PX4's gyro yaw rate, and a heading gate.
  - The fusion horizon (the `EKF2_GPS_DELAY` row above): 5.1 → 4.4 cm RMS.

The test drivers are scratchpad scripts. To repeat the test:
1. Start the sim with the `--print-params` values set and saved.
2. Run `19_lidar_px4_bridge.py --sim` (MAVLink 14540).
3. Wait about 60 s for TIMESYNC to converge.
4. Fly with `15_fly_plan.py <plan> --connect udpin:0.0.0.0:14550 --gcs-heartbeat`.

The bridge log's truth columns give the lidar's error. PX4's own log (`vehicle_local_position` vs `vehicle_local_position_groundtruth`) gives the EKF's.

### Smear and tilt in simulation (`lidar_sim.py`, `lidar_localize.py`, `17_lidar_study.py`)

Gazebo's GPU lidar runs at 3% of real time on the laptop, and it would not model per-beam timing anyway.

- **`lidar_sim.py`** casts every MS200K beam in 3D from the pose the drone had at that beam's own time. The scene contains:
  - wall boxes split around the window;
  - the tube's elliptical shells;
  - cylinder boulders;
  - nets, which return a beam 20% of the time;
  - the floor.
- **`lidar_localize.py`** matches each sweep to the map by Gauss–Newton on x, y and yaw. It can do two corrections:
  - **deskew:** place each point where the drone was when that beam fired, using the EKF velocity;
  - **tilt:** rotate each beam by PX4's roll and pitch, then drop the beams that end on the floor or above the walls.
- **The residual** is the point's distance to the face its beam actually crosses, found by intersecting the beam with the map's outline edges within ±25 cm of its end.
  - The nearest-surface distance doesn't work here. It pulls a point that sits past the middle of a 10 cm wall out through the back face, which settles one wall thickness (9–10 cm) off.
  - A 2 cm distance grid also can't represent the tube's 6 mm shell.
- **`17_lidar_study.py`** replays a SITL flight log through both:
  - each sweep starts from the previous estimate moved by the EKF velocity (truth + 5 cm/s);
  - roll and pitch come from PX4 (truth + 0.5°);
  - height from the rangefinder (truth + 2 cm).

Results (2026-10-28), position error RMS (max) in cm, every sweep of the course part, none lost. Rows are 10 Hz unless stated.

- **Realistic:** the 10 cm-margin plan, 2.2 m/s, tilt p95 11°.
- **Stress:** the full-speed plan, 2.3 m/s, tilt p95 21°, which crashed in the tube.

| Configuration | Realistic | Stress |
|---|---|---|
| frozen, level pose: the matcher alone | 0.1 (0.5) | 0.2 (0.7) |
| moving, matched as a snapshot | 5.3 (12.6) | 7.1 (12.7) |
| moving, deskewed with the true beam times | 0.5 (1.2) | 0.7 (1.7) |
| moving, deskewed with the stock driver's timing | 2.5 (6.6), yaw 1.0° | 2.5 (6.9), yaw 1.8° |
| tilted, matcher assumes level (9–10% of beams hit the floor) | 1.7 (5.3) | 3.2 (8.9) |
| tilted, compensated | 0.3 (0.8) | 0.5 (2.3) |
| both effects, naive | 5.2 (13.1) | 7.2 (12.7) |
| both effects, deskew + tilt compensation | **0.6 (1.5)** | **0.7 (2.1)** |
| same at 5 Hz / 15 Hz | 1.5 (3.0) / 0.4 (1.1) | 2.7 (7.0) / 0.6 (2.5) |
| course built 2 cm / 1° off the CAD | 1.3 (3.6) | 1.7 (3.6) |
| course built 4 cm / 2° off the CAD | 2.5 (7.2) | 3.2 (7.1) |

What this means:

- **Smear is the big effect.** At 2 m/s a sweep spans 20 cm of travel, and matching it as a snapshot is off by 5–7 cm (up to 13). Deskewing with the true beam times removes it. With the stock driver's timing, 2.5 cm and 1–2° of yaw remain, so the driver needs the timestamp patch.
- **Tilt matters less, but grows with aggression.** At 11–21° about a tenth of the beams land on the floor. Assuming a level scan costs 2–3 cm (up to 9). PX4's attitude fixes it.
- **Rotation rate:** keep 10 Hz. 5 Hz doubles to quadruples the error. 15 Hz gains a millimetre or two and has a third fewer points per sweep.
- **Once smear and tilt are corrected, the as-built course dominates.** Pieces placed 4 cm off the CAD cost 2.5–3 cm RMS and 7 cm worst case. Measure the real course on arrival and edit the map, or add a map-refinement step from scans taken on the pad (not built).
- **Not modelled:**
  - range bias;
  - mixed pixels at edges;
  - the drone's own frame blocking beams;
  - a non-flat floor;
  - real PX4 attitude errors beyond 0.5°.

  The sensor noise (1 cm) and dropout (2%) are assumptions.
