# Luxonis OAK-D S2 (USB) — Specification Sheet

**Scope.** This document covers the **OAK-D S2 USB model** only (EEPROM/PCBA board code **DM9098**, a board design Luxonis shares with the OAK-D W and OAK-D Pro, so the S2 identity comes from `getProductName()` / `getDeviceName()` rather than from the board code; shop SKUs **A00498** = Auto-focus, **A00566** = Fixed-focus). It does not cover the OAK-D S2 PoE, OAK-D W / Wide, OAK-D Pro, or OAK-D Lite, except where a difference is called out explicitly. Every fact carries the URL it came from; anything derived by arithmetic is labelled *derived*. See the **Confidence** section for the facts that could not be pinned to a primary Luxonis source or where Luxonis sources disagree with each other.

**Owner's unit.** The bench unit is the **OAK-D-S2-AF** (autofocus colour camera variant). The table below is what DepthAI 3.10.0 reported from that unit today on this Windows 11 laptop (device open measured at 3.4-4.3 s including ~0.5 s of enumeration, i.e. ~3 s of boot, over USB3). ## 2. RGB camera (CAM_A)

- **Sensor:** Sony IMX378, 1/2.3" (11.0 mm), 1.55 um pixels, 4056x3040, rolling shutter, colour, behind a 650 nm IR-cut filter (so it does not see IR). Sources: [Docs](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/products/OAK-D%20S2), [IMX378 page](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/sensors/IMX378), [Shop](https://shop.luxonis.com/products/oak-d-s2), [DS].
- **Frame rates on RVC2** ([IMX378 page](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/sensors/IMX378)): 12 MP 30 fps, 4K 30 fps, 1080p 60 fps, 2024x1520 85 fps, 1352x1012 52 fps (the unit's own mode list advertises 3840x2160 at 42 fps, [Meas]). The RVC2 encoder is rated 4K/30 and 1080p/60 ([RVC2 page](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/rvc/rvc2)), so 4K30 / 1080p60 is the practical streaming ceiling either way.
- **FOV differs by variant** because the AF and FF lens modules differ ([Docs](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/products/OAK-D%20S2), [Shop](https://shop.luxonis.com/products/oak-d-s2)): AF (PY004, EFL 4.81 mm) 78/66/54 deg; FF (PY052, EFL 4.74 mm) 82/69/55 deg (D/H/V).
- **Cross-check with the measured unit (*derived*):** HFOV = 2 * atan(2028 / 3065.9) = **67.0 deg**, VFOV = 2 * atan(1520 / 3066.0) = **52.7 deg**, DFOV 79.2 deg; EFL = 3065.9 px * 1.55 um = **4.75 mm**. The FOV figures (67.0 / 52.7 deg) match the AF lens (66 / 54 deg), not the FF lens (69 / 55 deg); the derived EFL of 4.75 mm sits between the two published EFLs (AF 4.81, FF 4.74 mm) and does not discriminate on its own. Together with `getProductName()` = `OAK-D-S2-AF` and `hasAutofocusIC = True` in the camera features, this confirms the AF variant.

### AF vs FF, and why it matters on a drone

Source for this subsection: the Luxonis focus-type guide, https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/sensors/focus-type, plus the [Shop](https://shop.luxonis.com/products/oak-d-s2) text.

- **Fixed-focus (FF):** "The Fixed-Focus variant has the lens fixed in place mechanically" ([Shop]); sharp from about **50 cm to infinity**. No moving parts.
- **Auto-focus (AF):** "the Auto-Focus has an electromagnet that controls the lens position dynamically" ([Shop]); sharp from **8 cm** ([Shop], [DS]) or **10 cm** ([focus guide]) **to infinity**. The AF module uses a **voice-coil motor (VCM)**: "In high vibrations, this electromagnetic force is overpowered and the lens vibrates all over the place, causing blurry/weird/'jello' frames" ([focus guide]).
- **Luxonis' recommendation:** "Overall, Auto-Focus cameras are more versatile and can be used for a wider range of applications, while Fixed-Focus cameras are more robust in high-vibration environments" ([focus guide](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/sensors/focus-type), Overview); "Fixed-Focus cameras are recommended for heavy-vibration applications, such as drones, lawnmowers, or vehicles" ([Shop]); FF is named for "drones, lawn mowers, heavy machinery, and motorcycles" ([focus guide]).
- **Manual focus is not a full fix:** the AF lens can be set to one of **255 manual positions** through the DepthAI API, but "in high-vibration environments, even if you set manual focus, the AF coil (which holds the lens) won't be able to keep the lens in place, which will result in a blurry image" ([focus guide]).
- **Focus hunting:** in continuous-AF mode the VCM re-seeks focus as the scene changes; on a moving airframe that means periodic soft frames and a changing effective focal length (a small zoom-like shift as the lens moves), which matters if RGB is used for detection or for RGB-depth alignment. Setting a fixed manual focus position removes the hunting; it does not remove the vibration-induced lens motion above. (This paragraph is the practical consequence of the Luxonis statements above, not a separate Luxonis quote.)
- **What this does NOT affect:** stereo depth comes from the two fixed-focus, global-shutter OV9282 cameras; the AF module only affects the colour stream and anything computed from it. Note the vibration test results list "Camera focus - Passed" for the tested (OAK-D Pro) unit ([Vibration tolerance](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/environmental-specifications/vibration-tolerance)); that test is a sinusoidal 5-200 Hz sweep at 50 m/s^2, which is not the same as continuous multirotor prop-wash vibration.

## 3. Stereo cameras (CAM_B left, CAM_C right)

- **Sensor:** OmniVision OV9282, 1/4" (6.3 mm), 3 um pixels, 1280x800 monochrome, **global shutter**, fixed focus 19.6 cm to infinity, EFL 2.35 mm, F/2.0, 650 nm IR-cut filter. Sources: [Docs](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/products/OAK-D%20S2), [OV9282 page](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/sensors/OV9282), [Shop](https://shop.luxonis.com/products/oak-d-s2), [DS].
- **FOV:** DFOV 89.5 deg / HFOV 80 deg / VFOV 55 deg ([Docs], [Shop], [DS]). Luxonis' FOV calculator link on the docs page uses horizontalFov=80, verticalFov=55 at 1280x800. [Meas] the unit's calibration gives HFOV 77.4 / 77.5 deg and VFOV 53.2 / 53.3 deg for CAM_B / CAM_C (*derived* as 2 * atan((W/2) / fx), a pinhole estimate that ignores barrel distortion and so reads a little narrower than the published 80 deg).
- **Frame rates on RVC2:** 129 fps at 800P, 143 fps at 720P, 255 fps at 400P ([OV9282 page](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/sensors/OV9282)); the datasheet's "1280x800@120FPS" is the older figure.
- **Global vs rolling shutter:** the stereo pair is global shutter, so both images are exposed simultaneously across the whole frame; there is no row-wise skew from vibration or fast yaw, which is what keeps stereo matching valid on a moving platform. The RGB IMX378 is rolling shutter and will show skew/"jello" under the same motion.
- **Not IR sensitive:** unlike the OAK-D Pro's stereo pair (940 nm notch filter, see section 10), the S2's stereo cameras have a 650 nm IR-cut filter ([Shop]); [DS] table says "IR sensitive: No". (The datasheet's Features bullet "global shutter cameras with no IR filter" contradicts its own sensor table and the shop; see Confidence.)
- **Measured:** CAM_B and CAM_C both enumerate as OV9282 1280x800 MONO on the owner's unit, matching all sources. Calibrated intrinsics at 1280x800: CAM_B fx = 799.13, fy = 798.90, cx = 649.47, cy = 391.54 px; CAM_C fx = 797.54, fy = 797.47, cx = 652.93, cy = 368.24 px (these feed the MinZ figures in section 4).

## 4. Stereo depth: baseline, MinZ, MaxZ, accuracy

**Baseline = 75 mm.** [Shop](https://shop.luxonis.com/products/oak-d-s2) states "Base line: 75 mm" and "a baseline of 7.5cm"; the [depth accuracy page](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/depth/depth-accuracy) groups the OAK-D S2 under "800P, 75mm baseline"; the owner's unit calibration returns **7.5 cm**. The [Docs product page](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/products/OAK-D%20S2) prints "baseline of 75cm", which is a typo (an OAK-D S2 is only 97 mm wide).

**Luxonis formulas** (from https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/depth/configuring-stereo-depth):

- `depth_cm = focal_length_in_pixels * baseline_cm / disparity_px`
- MinZ: the disparity search is 0-95 px in normal mode; **extended disparity** "increases the levels of disparity to 191 from the standard 96 pixels, thereby 1/2-ing the minimum depth"; halving the resolution (400P) halves the focal length in pixels and so also halves MinZ. Luxonis' worked example (for the original OAK-D, 882.5 px focal length at 1280 px, HFOV 71.9 deg): 882.5 * 7.5 / 95 = 69.7 cm; with extended disparity 882.5 * 7.5 / 190 = 34.8 cm; with 400P + extended about 17.5 cm, "though at such short distances the minimum depth is limited by focal length, which is 19.6cm" (the OV9282 fixed-focus lens).
- MaxZ: `Dm = (baseline/2) * tan((90 - HFOV/HPixels) * pi/180)`, which the page calls "an approximation" (it does not say, and it is not, the distance at which disparity falls to one pixel: by the depth formula above, 1 px disparity for the OAK-D example is 882.5 * 7.5 / 1 = 66 m, whereas the formula gives the 38.25 m the page prints for the OAK-D).
- **Subpixel mode** adds fractional disparity bits (3, 4 or 5 bits = 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 px): 754 / 1506 / 3010 unique depth values instead of 96; "Subpixel mode improves the precision and is especially useful for long-range measurements" ([StereoDepth node page](https://docs.luxonis.com/software-v3/depthai/depthai-components/nodes/stereo_depth)); the configuring-stereo-depth page's own wording is "If greater precision for long range measurements is required, consider enabling Subpixel Disparity or using a larger baseline distance between mono cameras". `StereoDepth.setSubpixel()` / `setSubpixelFractionalBits()` / `setExtendedDisparity()` all exist in the installed depthai 3.10.0.
- "Disparity and depth are inversely related. As disparity decreases, the depth increases exponentially" and "Depth accuracy decreases exponentially with the distance from the camera".

**Luxonis family-boilerplate MinZ** ([Docs] / [Shop]; the same numbers are printed for the OAK-D Pro, OAK-D S2 PoE and, on the Docs side, even the OAK-D Lite, so no Luxonis page gives an S2-specific MinZ):

| Mode | Docs page | Shop page |
|---|---|---|
| 800P, normal disparity | ~80 cm | ~70 cm |
| 400P, normal; or 800P + extended disparity | ~40 cm | ~35 cm |
| 400P + extended disparity | ~20 cm | ~20 cm (lens floor 19.6 cm) |

**Values for the owner's unit from the same formula (*derived* from the unit's own calibration, not a Luxonis claim):** the calibration gives CAM_B fx = **799.1 px** at 1280x800 (CAM_C 797.5 px; HFOV from intrinsics 77.4 / 77.5 deg; 399.6 px at 640x400). Then MinZ = 799.1 * 7.5 / 95 = **63.1 cm** (800P), **31.5 cm** (800P + extended, or 400P), **15.8 cm** (400P + extended, so lens-limited to 19.6 cm); the 1 px-disparity distance by the depth formula is 799.1 * 7.5 / 1 = **59.9 m**, and Luxonis' approximate MaxZ formula gives Dm = 3.75 * tan((90 - 77.4/1280) deg) = **35.5 m**.

**Cross-check from the published 80 deg HFOV (*derived*):** f_px at 1280 px = 640 / tan(40 deg) = **762.7 px** (381.4 px at 640 px), giving MinZ 60.2 cm (800P) / 30.1 cm / 15.1 cm, Dm = 34.4 m and a 1 px-disparity distance of 57.2 m, all about 5 % below the calibrated values (the pinhole HFOV from intrinsics ignores lens distortion, so it reads narrower than the published 80 deg). Dm and the 1 px distance are different quantities and the Luxonis approximation is the more conservative. The Shop figures (70/35/20) are the original OAK-D example numbers (71.9 deg HFOV); the Docs figures (80/40/20) are rounder still. Evidence that both sets are template values rather than S2 statements: the Docs sentence ("Ideal range: ~80cm - 12m", "MinZ: ~20cm (400P + extended disp), ~40cm (400P OR 800P + extended), ~80cm (800P)") is printed verbatim on the OAK-D Pro, OAK-D S2 PoE and OAK-D Lite product pages (the Lite has a 480P OV7251 stereo pair behind a 73 deg HFOV lens, so the same numbers cannot be exact for both), and the Shop block ("Ideal depth range: 70cm - 12m", "Maximum effective depth range: 30m", "MinZ: ~20cm (400P and extended), ~35cm (400P OR 800P, extended), ~70cm (800P)") is identical on the OAK-D Pro and OAK-D S2 PoE shop pages. Treat **~63 cm** as the 800P near limit for this unit (Luxonis' boilerplate 70-80 cm is conservative for it), and measure against a target before relying on anything nearer.

**Depth accuracy claims** ([Docs](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/products/OAK-D%20S2), [Shop](https://shop.luxonis.com/products/oak-d-s2), [Depth accuracy](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/depth/depth-accuracy)): median absolute depth error **< 2 % below 4 m**, **< 4 % at 4-7 m**, **< 6 % at 7-10 m** (the accuracy page says 7-10.5 m). Test method on that page: highest stereo resolution, subpixel on with 5 fractional bits, ROI on a textured board, outliers (top/bottom 5 %) removed, median of remaining depth. The measured device was an **OAK-D Pro**, not an S2: the page's Parameters section reads "OAK-D Pro: Using `depthai` library, subpixel mode, LR-mode, and with 800P resolution. No post-processing filters." (same OV9282 pair and 75 mm baseline, 940 nm notch filter instead of IR-cut; the page notes that on its textured test board the Pro's dot projector "won't make a difference"). The S2 inherits the band only by membership of the "800P, 75mm baseline distance OAKs" group, which also lists the gen1 OAK-D (71.9 deg HFOV lens), OAK-D Pro, OAK-D Pro PoE, OAK-D S2 PoE and OAK-D PoE, so the band cannot be exact for every lens in the group. The configuring-stereo-depth page adds: at ~70 cm with good texture, "below 2% error ... so +/- 1.5cm".

**Depth quantisation vs distance (*derived* from the Luxonis depth formula, S2 at 800P, f = 762.7 px from the published 80 deg HFOV, B = 7.5 cm; dZ = Z^2 * d_disp / (f * B)):**

| Distance | 1 px disparity error | 1/8 px (3-bit subpixel) | 1/32 px (5-bit subpixel) | Luxonis bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 m | 1.7 cm | 0.2 cm | 0.05 cm | < 2 cm |
| 2 m | 7.0 cm | 0.9 cm | 0.2 cm | < 4 cm |
| 4 m | 28 cm | 3.5 cm | 0.9 cm | < 8-16 cm |
| 5 m | 44 cm | 5.5 cm | 1.4 cm | < 20 cm |
| 7 m | 86 cm | 10.7 cm | 2.7 cm | < 28 cm |
| 10 m | 175 cm | 22 cm | 5.5 cm | < 60 cm |
| 12 m | 252 cm | 31 cm | 7.9 cm | n/a (no Luxonis figure beyond 10.5 m; 72 cm would be the 6 % band extrapolated) |

Without subpixel, one disparity step is already 44 cm at 5 m and 1.75 m at 10 m, which is why Luxonis' own accuracy tests always enable subpixel. With the unit's own calibrated focal length (799.1 px) every entry is about 4.5 % smaller (e.g. 42 cm instead of 44 cm per disparity pixel at 5 m); the spec-HFOV table is kept as the conservative case.

## 5. IMU

- **Nominal part:** BNO086, 9-axis (3-axis accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, with on-chip sensor fusion). Sources: [Docs](https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/products/OAK-D%20S2), [Shop](https://shop.luxonis.com/products/oak-d-s2).
- **Supply-chain substitution:** "Due to supply chain issues, most of the OAK camera that were manufactured between Q2 2021 and Q2 2023 have integrated BMI270 - 6-axis IMU instead" ([Docs]). The BMI270 has no magnetometer and no on-chip fusion.
- **Capability difference** (DepthAI v3 IMU node page, https://docs.luxonis.com/software-v3/depthai/depthai-components/nodes/imu/): RVC2 devices carry either BNO08X or BMI270. BNO08X supports raw/uncalibrated/calibrated accelerometer and gyroscope, magnetometer, and fused outputs (ROTATION_VECTOR, GAME_ROTATION_VECTOR, GEOMAGNETIC_ROTATION_VECTOR, ARVR-stabilised variants, GRAVITY, LINEAR_ACCELERATION). BMI270 supports only accelerometer and gyroscope streams (raw, uncalibrated, calibrated), no magnetometer and no fused orientation. Requested rates round up on BNO08X and down on BMI270; the page's example uses ACCELEROMETER_RAW at 480 Hz and GYROSCOPE_RAW at 400 Hz.
- **How to tell which IMU you have:** call `device.getConnectedIMU()` (returns e.g. `"BNO086"` or `"BMI270"`) and `device.getIMUFirmwareVersion()`. This is exactly what the official v3 examples do: `examples/python/IMU/imu_version.py` prints both, and `examples/python/IMU/imu_rotation_vector.py` checks `not device.getConnectedIMU().startswith("BMI")` before enabling `dai.IMUSensor.ROTATION_VECTOR` at 200 Hz (https://github.com/luxonis/depthai-core/tree/main/examples/python/IMU). Both methods exist on `dai.Device` in the installed depthai 3.10.0 (verified by introspection). The IMU node also exposes `enableFirmwareUpdate(bool)`.
- **Measured:** the owner's unit reports **BNO086**, firmware 3.9.9, so it is the full 9-axis part and ROTATION_VECTOR is available. This unit is therefore not one of the BMI270-substituted batches (EEPROM batchTime 1764034136 = 2025-11-25 UTC, well outside the Q2 2021-Q2 2023 window).

## 7. Interface and USB bandwidth

- **Connector:** single USB Type-C carrying power and data; no barrel jack ([DS]: "The system is powered over a USB Type-C"; [Shop]: "USB-C (USB 3.2 Gen1, 5 Gbps) for power and connectivity").
- **Link speed, what the sources say:** [Shop] "USB 3.2 Gen1, 5 Gbps"; [DS] description "USB 3.1 Gen1" (its Features bullet says "USB3.1, gen2 10gbps"); [Docs] "USB 2/3 (up to 10 Gbps)". The owner's unit normally enumerates as `UsbSpeed.SUPER`, which is the 5 Gbps class (depthai's enum has a separate `SUPER_PLUS` for 10 Gbps), so **plan on a 5 Gbps link**.
- **Usable throughput:** Luxonis' USB guide (https://docs.luxonis.com/hardware/platform/deploy/usb-deployment-guide) says a healthy connection shows "USB3 downlink should be about 2.5 Gbps" with latency under 0.5 s.
- **USB2 fallback:** the device drops to USB2 (480 Mbps theoretical) "If you have are using an USB2 cable/USB2 port, have a bad USB3 cable, or are using longer cable (eg. larger than 2.5 meters)"; "Only the booted device enumerates as USB3. If it stays at USB2 speed after the app starts, the most common cause is the host-side USB port or cable"; cables over 2 m should be active USB3 cables; USB3 cables are "usually blue inside the USB-A connector"; a screw-locking USB3 cable is recommended where the connection can be disturbed ([USB guide]). Measured today: in five back-to-back boots on this laptop during the IMU test, one enumerated as `UsbSpeed.HIGH` (USB 2, 480 Mbps) with no hardware change between boots; the other four, and every boot in the other scripts, were `SUPER`. The USB2 fallback is therefore intermittent, not only a bad-cable symptom, which is why the start-up check is mandatory: check `device.getUsbSpeed()` at start-up and refuse to fly on anything but `SUPER`.
- **Power over USB:** "USB3 is capable of providing up to 900mA of current, while USB2 is capable of providing up to 500mA"; "OAK cameras draw between 500mA and 900mA, depending on the model and the current workload" ([USB guide]). The datasheet's 7.5 W maximum requirement (1.5 A at 5 V; a figure from the datasheet template shared with the OAK-D Pro, see section 8) exceeds a nominal USB3 port budget, so the host port/rail matters (see section 8).
- **Raw stream bit rates (*derived*, uncompressed, 30 fps):** mono 1280x800 GRAY8 = 0.25 Gb/s each; depth 1280x800 uint16 = 0.49 Gb/s; RGB 1080p NV12 = 0.75 Gb/s; RGB 4K NV12 = 2.99 Gb/s; RGB 12 MP NV12 = 4.44 Gb/s. So left + right + depth + 1080p colour (about 1.7 Gb/s) fits the ~2.5 Gb/s USB3 budget, a raw 4K stream alone does not, and on a USB2 fallback (0.48 Gb/s) only something like 400P depth (0.12) + two 400P mono (0.12) + 640x480 colour (0.11) fits. Use the on-device H.265/MJPEG encoder for anything that is only recorded.

## 14. Implications for a 7-inch quad with a Jetson Orin Nano host

Facts used: baseline 75 mm, stereo HFOV 80 deg / VFOV 55 deg, 800P global-shutter stereo, MinZ ~63 cm at 800P from the unit's own calibration (Luxonis boilerplate 70-80 cm; ~32 cm at 400P or with extended disparity, 20 cm lens floor), Luxonis accuracy bounds (< 2 % to 4 m, < 4 % to 7 m, < 6 % to 10 m), Luxonis "ideal range" 12 m, USB3 downlink ~2.5 Gb/s, and the derived quantisation table in section 4.

- **Usable obstacle-avoidance range is roughly 0.65 m to 5-7 m, not 12 m.** Below ~0.65 m (800P; 63 cm from this unit's calibration) the camera returns nothing; a 7-inch quad is ~0.3 m motor-to-motor, so an obstacle that is inside the blind zone is already within one airframe length. Running the stereo pair at 400P (or 800P with extended disparity) pulls the near limit to ~0.3 m at the cost of half the angular resolution per disparity step (400P) or twice the disparity search (extended). Beyond ~5 m the depth steps get coarse (44 cm per disparity pixel at 5 m without subpixel, 5.5 cm with 3-bit subpixel) and Luxonis' own bound loosens to 4-6 %; treat 4-7 m as the planning horizon and 7-12 m as "something is there" only. Always enable subpixel; Luxonis' accuracy numbers assume it.
- **Speed budget follows from that horizon.** At 5 m/s the quad crosses a 4 m horizon in 0.8 s and a 7 m horizon in 1.4 s; at 10 m/s those become 0.4 s and 0.7 s; at 15 m/s 0.27 s and 0.47 s. Stereo at 30 fps plus USB and host latency (Luxonis' "under 0.5 s" figure is a health threshold, not a target; well-tuned pipelines are much lower) eats a meaningful fraction of the 10 m/s budget. Avoidance flight should be planned at low single-digit m/s unless the pipeline latency is measured and small.
- **FOV coverage.** 80 deg x 55 deg gives a 1.7 x 1.0 m window at 1 m, 3.4 x 2.1 m at 2 m, 8.4 x 5.2 m at 5 m (*derived*). Anything outside +/-40 deg of the optical axis is invisible, so lateral/vertical drift and yaw rates must be limited, or the camera tilted toward the direction of travel. The 55 deg vertical FOV is the tighter one: at 2 m the camera only sees +/-1.05 m vertically, so pitching the quad forward at speed pushes the ground into the frame and the path out of it.
- **Global shutter stereo is the right sensor for a vibrating airframe;** the rolling-shutter RGB is not, and the owner's AF colour module is explicitly the variant Luxonis advises against for drones (section 2). Depth and any NN run on the mono/depth streams are unaffected. If the RGB stream is needed for detection, lock a manual focus position, isolate the camera from the frame with soft mounts, and expect residual VCM blur; otherwise swap to the FF variant (A00566) for the airframe.
- **Passive stereo needs texture and light.** No projector means blank walls, plain floors, sky and dusk produce depth holes; do not rely on the depth map for those surfaces, and use the confidence/left-right-check outputs to mask them.
- **USB on the Jetson:** the Orin Nano dev kit's Type-A ports are USB 3.2 hosts (NVIDIA specification, not a Luxonis statement), so the link will be the same 5 Gbps class measured here (one of five back-to-back boots here still fell back to USB2, so the check below must gate every start-up). Per Luxonis, keep the cable short (< 2 m, active if longer), USB3-rated and preferably screw-locking, and verify `device.getUsbSpeed() == dai.UsbSpeed.SUPER` at start-up; a USB2 fallback (0.48 Gb/s theoretical) cannot carry 800P depth plus colour. A comfortable USB3 budget is stereo left + right (0.25 Gb/s each) + 16-bit depth (0.49 Gb/s) + 1080p NV12 colour (0.75 Gb/s) = ~1.7 Gb/s under the ~2.5 Gb/s Luxonis figure; do not ship raw 4K or 12 MP over the link, use the on-device H.265 encoder or a lower colour resolution, and send only what the host consumes (e.g. depth + one mono for VIO rather than both monos + disparity + depth).
- **Power on the Jetson:** budget 1.5 A at 5 V for the camera (the datasheet's 7.5 W max, a template value, see section 8) on top of the Jetson's own draw; running the stereo pair at 400P/30 fps and skipping the encoder keeps it at the low end of the ~3-5 W typical running load (section 8). Compute-wise, let the RVC2 do stereo matching and any small NN on-device (1.4 TOPS) so the Orin Nano only receives depth, which keeps its USB and CPU load low.

## 15. Confidence notes

Facts that could not be confirmed from a primary Luxonis source, or where Luxonis sources disagree:

1. **Baseline "75cm" on the docs product page** is a typo (the same sentence, "This OAK camera has a baseline of 75cm", is printed on the OAK-D Pro, OAK-D S2 PoE and OAK-D Lite pages, so it is a template typo); three other Luxonis sources and the unit's calibration say 75 mm. High confidence in 75 mm.
2. **MinZ figures:** Docs (80/40/20 cm) and Shop (70/35/20 cm) differ, and neither matches the S2's own geometry (the unit's calibrated CAM_B focal length, 799.1 px, gives 63.1 / 31.5 / 15.8-19.6 cm by Luxonis' own formula, and the published 80 deg HFOV gives 60.2 / 30.1 / 15.1-19.6 cm; section 4). Both are product-family boilerplate: the Docs block is printed verbatim on the OAK-D Pro, OAK-D S2 PoE and OAK-D Lite pages, and the Shop block on the OAK-D Pro and S2 PoE shop pages (it equals the gen1 OAK-D worked example on the configuring-stereo-depth page). No Luxonis page gives an S2-specific MinZ. The 63 cm figure is computed from the unit's calibration, not measured against a target. Medium-high confidence; measure before relying on anything under ~0.7 m.
3. **Ideal range 80 cm vs 70 cm** (Docs vs Shop) and **accuracy band 7-10 m vs 7-10.5 m** (product pages vs depth-accuracy page): minor inconsistencies between Luxonis pages; the bounds are median errors from Luxonis' test rig (measured on an OAK-D Pro, same OV9282 pair and 75 mm baseline but a 940 nm notch filter instead of IR-cut, and applied to the S2 by group membership together with the gen1 OAK-D; section 4), not guarantees.
4. **USB generation:** Docs says "up to 10 Gbps", datasheet Features says "USB3.1 gen2 10gbps", but the datasheet description ("USB 3.1 Gen1"), the Shop ("USB 3.2 Gen1, 5 Gbps") and the measured `UsbSpeed.SUPER` all indicate a 5 Gbps link. High confidence in 5 Gbps.
5. **Stereo IR filter:** the datasheet Features bullet says the OV9282 pair has "no IR filter", contradicting the datasheet's own sensor table ("IR sensitive: No"), the Shop table (IR-cut 650 nm) and the docs page ("Infrared: No"). The OAK-D Pro datasheet ([Pro DS], same "Dec, 2021" template) has the identical Features bullet and explains it: "OAK-D Pro doesn't include IR filter on mono cameras (production version will have notch IR filters at 940nm)"; its sensor table says "IR sensitive: Yes" where the S2 sheet says "No". The bullet is therefore Pro wording carried into the S2 sheet. High confidence the S2's stereo pair is IR-cut.
6. **Frame rates:** datasheet (IMX378 "4056x3040@60fps", OV9282 "1280x800@120FPS") vs the sensor pages (12 MP 30 fps, 800P 129 fps) vs Shop ("up to 60 FPS" / "up to 255 FPS"). Both datasheet rows appear verbatim in the OAK-D Pro datasheet ([Pro DS]), i.e. they are template values. The sensor pages are the most specific and most recent; medium-high confidence.
7. **AF near focus:** 8 cm ([Shop], [DS]) vs 10 cm ([focus guide]). Either way it is irrelevant on a drone.
8. **Weight 91 g** comes only from the Shop page (a primary Luxonis source, but a single one) and is a family-level figure rather than a per-product measurement (see "Weight" in section 1); weigh the bench unit for an actual value. The Shopify variant record carries a shipping-weight field of 300 (grams, presumably, boxed) which should not be confused with the device mass.
9. **"Maximum effective depth range: 30m"** appears only on the Shop page (and identically on the OAK-D Pro and S2 PoE shop pages); Luxonis' approximate MaxZ formula gives ~34-36 m for the S2 geometry (published HFOV / unit calibration; the 1 px-disparity distance by the depth formula is ~57-60 m; section 4) and no Luxonis accuracy figure is given beyond 10.5 m. Treat 30 m as detection-only.
10. **Datasheet power table** (4/6/7.5 W min/typ/max) was read from a text extraction of the PDF; the row/column pairing was checked with a reading-order extraction and is consistent, but the PDF was not rendered visually. The identical table (VBUS 3.5-5.5 V, 2 A, 0-60 C; 5 V / 5.25 V; P 4 / 6 / 7.5 W; PIDLE 2.5 W; TA 50 C) appears in the OAK-D Pro datasheet ([Pro DS]), so it is a template (see "Power (datasheet)" in section 1). Medium-high confidence in the extraction; low confidence that the table is S2-specific.
11. **Vibration test** was run on an OAK-D Pro and applied "indirectly" to the S2 by Luxonis; it is a sinusoidal-sweep standard, not a multirotor vibration profile. The Luxonis AF-under-vibration warnings (section 2) are qualitative; no Luxonis source quantifies the vibration level at which the VCM fails.
12. **Jetson Orin Nano USB port class** is from NVIDIA documentation, not Luxonis, and is stated here only as context.
13. The EEPROM batchTime of the owner's unit is 1764034136 (Unix time = 2025-11-25 UTC; the rev string R9M2E9 is not a date), which places it well outside the Q2 2021-Q2 2023 BMI270 window, so the BMI270 caveat is resolved both by direct measurement (BNO086 present) and by the batch timestamp.
